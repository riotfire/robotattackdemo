# Robot Attack! — first five waves fun plan

October 25, 2026. Proposed design, based on the current working tree at HEAD `23b8683`, including its uncommitted changes. This is a new opening-specific plan; it does not silently replace the GDD or the accepted roadmap.

**Status, October 26, 2026: built as a six-wave opening.** The [phase plan](PHASE_CHECKPOINT_PROGRESSION_PLAN.md) (step 4) moved this arc onto three-wave phases, and the recalibrated [playtest simulation](PLAYTEST_SIMULATION_REPORT.md) (branch `claude/beautiful-yalow-10a844`) supplied the rosters. Waves 1–6 are now: Nibblers (1); the Clanker trio, the pistol and the teaching thief (2); the 700 HP first Foreman (3); one Roof Lobber on the tool shed (4); the Rung Runner on the silo ladder, a Lobber on the barn roof and a late, announced raid from a third direction (5); and a second Foreman with a clod-throw phase (6). The rosters are data in `data/source/opening/`, and [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.md) section 4.13 lists the work.

Built from this plan: groups released by time or after an earlier group, live caps that hold a group back instead of dumping it later, arrivals 45 m out, and waves that clear as soon as nothing is left to spawn or alive. The first Foreman has a 1.2 s slam tell, a 2.5 s recovery with a ×3 vent, and no random trait; its defeat calls off the adds still to come and marks the survivors. The pistol kit and a free tool (Barn Blaster or Haymaker Maul, now after wave 2) are first-completion grants that settle once per profile. The ready bonus is a fixed 5 Al, paid after the wave clears and only above the best wave. The 30 s break has a quick-buy panel (Tab): three affordable suggestions that name a real breakpoint, a pinned goal, and Ready. Lanes are named by landmark, debut captions show once per profile, cracks stay on the ground through wave 2 (already in place), and at clear the worst leak is pointed out with its time to seal, with a relief chime when it's sealed.

**Reversed after the user's playtest (2026-10-26):** a quick-buy menu usable anywhere "takes out the fun of traveling". Intermissions are 90 s again with shopping at the shed; the quick-buy panel, Ready-from-anywhere, pinned goals and the Relaxed setting are removed; no wave hands over an item (no pistol kit, free tool or free Spring Trap copy); defenses are placed freely instead of on authored sites; and the opening is lighter (Clanker pairs, a 350 HP first Foreman the wrench can beat, a 900 HP second one). See ROADMAP section 4.13, "Playtest rework".

Changed from this plan: the full shop doesn't pause the clock or the farm (the [defense proposal](DEFENSE_BUILDING_AND_COMBAT_PROGRESSION_PROPOSAL.md) supersedes that), and the relaxed setting is a 90 s break rather than 120 s or untimed. Not built yet: the shorter tutorial, camera and crowd framing, offscreen threat arrows, melee aim assist and a reach cue, the encounter-timing logs, and the eight-player validation round.

**Make the opening feel like: “Bonk a robot. Get a gun. Learn my farm's shortcuts. Save a stolen sack. Outwit a ridiculous boss.”** The existing farm, robot personalities, salvage bursts, and climbing routes support that promise. The opening needs a deliberately staged sequence that reliably delivers it.

**Revised direction:** the jetpack is a substantial long-term reward that unlocks an aerial combat style. Every rooftop is already reachable without it. The first five waves must be rewarding through ground combat, climbing, rooftop positions, and ziplines. The previous early-jetpack recommendation was based on the wrong dependency and is withdrawn. See [the jetpack gameplay and reward plan](JETPACK_GAMEPLAY_AND_REWARD_PLAN.md) for the replacement design; the original measurements below remain evidence, not justification for accelerating the unlock.

**What I ran and what the evidence means**

I launched an isolated graphical run, tried the opening wrench combat, inspected the incoming-wave cards, damage warnings, repair labels, and defeat screen, and used development controls to inspect later introductions. I then ran all five real wave schedules in an isolated observation fixture, and captured all five rendered waves. The fixture grants god mode, floors Structure and Grain at 50%, resets the silo between waves, and removes remaining robots after each spawn window plus five seconds. It does not fight or buy upgrades. This is an assisted walkthrough and mechanical audit, **not an unassisted five-wave completion or a measured novice difficulty test**.

The schedule audit covers seeds 1–100; the full runtime observation uses seed 7. Rendered captures use Morning/Clear, an explicitly positioned gameplay camera, and 4× simulation speed. They establish composition and visibility problems, not reaction times, normal-speed feel, or performance. I inspected the audio implementation but did not evaluate an audible mix. All original gameplay files and the user's normal profile were left intact. Reproduction details and limitations are in [the evidence notes](audit-evidence/2026-10-25-opening/README.md).

**The proposed playable arc**

Numbers below are initial tuning values, not proven balance. Combat durations include arrivals and finishing the last enemies. Introductions have authored positions and ordering; robot cosmetics and constrained lane choices remain seeded.

| Wave | Player feeling and new decision | Starting roster / routes | Encounter target | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — First Bonks | “I can do this.” Close the distance, land two swings, see a satisfying breakup. | 8 Nibblers; one clearly visible lane; no thieves. Three groups: 2 → 3 → 3. Target at most 4 live nearby. | 45–55 s; first visible bot within 3 s of siren ending, first melee opportunity within 6 s. | A guaranteed Rivet Pistol kit and a short satisfying victory beat. |
| 2 — Stop That Sack | “This new tool changes things.” Shoot a slower squad, then intercept one obvious thief using a known ground shortcut. | 7 Nibblers + 1 Clanker trio = 10. Two lanes introduced sequentially. Exactly one teaching thief, delayed until the first squad is resolved. | 55–65 s; at most 6 live nearby; one readable 5–7 s respite before the theft beat. | Useful parts for an affordable upgrade; reveal the ladder/roof/zipline route that will matter next. No flight grant. |
| 3 — Take the High Ground | “Knowing this farm gives me an advantage.” Choose a ground firing angle or climb to a useful roof, remove the lobber, and return by a taught route. | 6 Nibblers + 2 Clanker trios + 1 Roof Lobber = 13. Two lanes. Lobber uses a designated roof with a readable existing approach and a viable ground firing angle. | 60–70 s; first vertical lesson has no competing large attack group. Target at most 7 live nearby. Measure the real climb-and-return time before fixing the respite. | One free choice: Barn Blaster or Haymaker Maul. Show their tactical difference and let the player try the choice safely. |
| 4 — My Farm, My Route | “I have a plan.” Use the chosen tool against groups; choose repair versus interception. | 8 Nibblers + 3 Clanker trios + 1 Roof Lobber = 18. Two main lanes; announce a third-direction raid late, while one main lane rests. One thief maximum. | 65–75 s; target at most 9 live nearby. Create two pressure peaks separated by 6–8 s of genuine relief. | A clear preview of the Foreman and time to choose one affordable improvement. |
| 5 — Foreman's First Visit | “I learned this, and it works.” Read a slam, evade, punish the exposed vent, manage small adds. | 1 Foreman + 6 Nibblers + 2 Clanker trios = 13 total. Two lanes, no drone, no lobber, no random trait. | 80–100 s total, roughly 45–60 s engaged with the boss. Target at most 6 adds alive and never release a new group during the first slam lesson. | Crown pops off, a clear grain-saved result, guaranteed reward, and a player-controlled choice to continue into wave six. |

This creates approximately **7–9 minutes from the wave-one bell to wave-five victory**, with four 30-second preparations and short clear beats. Including a focused prologue, aim for approximately 9–11 minutes. The current no-bell path from the tutorial bell takes approximately 13 minutes before adding mop-up or the prologue itself.

**How the beats should work**

Keep wave one's melee identity. The tutorial should provide the wrench and one meaningful practice fight quickly. Target 60–90 seconds for a player following prompts, while remaining untimed. Remove the required artificial damage/heal detour from the critical path; teach healing when health is actually missing. Offer repair only when the practice bot opens a leak. Retain the catwalk and zipline as ordinary traversal lessons. If the full tour makes the prologue drag, move that tour into an untimed, player-controlled preparation after wave two, with optional exploration earlier. This teaches existing movement without promising or requiring a jetpack. These are explicit proposed changes to Morning Chores, not descriptions of current behavior. The 9–11 minute session target excludes deliberate extra exploration or paused lessons.

The first two wave-one enemies should enter the same view as the silo, with enough separation to learn the swing. Keep the current two-hit Nibbler breakpoint. Give the second hit a distinct impact sound, brief directional squash, and chunky salvage. Test a modest melee aim assist and a clearer reach cue before increasing damage. Do not make the whole farm freeze on every ordinary hit; reserve strong emphasis for a finisher or boss defeat.

The teaching thief should be unmistakable: visible sack, distinct motion, a short caption, and a marked interception route. Give it a recoverable head start rather than silently reducing its health. The farmer runs at 7 m/s and a base Nibbler at 8 m/s; a straight chase from behind is a poor lesson. Place its first escape route across the player's view and give enough pistol time to intercept. Missing the sack should cost grain but should not derail the rest of the tutorial arc.

Wave three should reward learning the farm. Mark a ladder or climbable route, show the useful roof angle, and make the return route legible. The lobber should glow before its first throw and display a projectile landing marker. Shooting it from the ground and using an elevated position are both legitimate tactics. Compare their exposure, travel time, and sight lines; do not force a roof visit when the encounter offers no reason to take it. No wave-one-through-five objective, crack, enemy, or escape route depends on flight.

Prototype the first Foreman at **700 HP**, with a 600–800 tuning range. Keep ordinary damage possible throughout. Give the slam a clear 1.2-second anticipation and a roughly 2.5-second recovery that exposes the back vent. Test a 3× vent multiplier only during recovery, with a visual distinction from ordinary weak points. Start with a solo entrance and one readable slam; add squads only after the player has had that opportunity. Prioritize holding the boss away from the silo through aggro and positioning. At death, cancel unspawned adds and mark any living survivors immediately; target no more than six seconds of cleanup. Tune to actual engagement time using the guaranteed opening kit, then check upgraded profiles separately.

**Preparation, rewards, and stress**

Introduce a compact panel with three useful, affordable suggestions after each wave, while retaining access to the full pegboard. Show an actual behavioral benefit or a changed breakpoint: “two shots instead of three” where the calculation is true. Avoid presenting a dozen tiny percentages as the main reward. Let the player pin one later goal.

Default preparation is 30 seconds, including the three-second siren. A visible Ready action can ring the bell remotely once the player closes shopping. Keep the physical bell as a delightful world interaction. Opening the full shop in solo pauses both the countdown and world simulation, including grain leakage, and clearly says so. Offer a 120-second or untimed relaxed setting without reducing rewards. Do not shorten today's timer until this buying flow is available; this preserves the intent behind roadmap decision D1.

The proposed pistol kit is a first-completion progression grant, not credits that can accidentally be spent on something else. If already owned, show the completion payoff without granting another saleable kit. The tool choice after wave three grants one previously unowned tool, with a fixed small fallback reward if both are owned. The jetpack remains a separate long-term project, with its timing and cost tuned after its gameplay is proven. Keep existing earned upgrades intact. Add explicit save flags and migration behavior; never assume a veteran profile is a fresh balance test.

During waves one and two, restrict newly opened cracks to ground sockets while the player learns repair. Introduce a catwalk crack after teaching its ladder and landing, with enough respite for the climb, repair, and return. Validate those routes without a jetpack. When combat ends, point to the worst leak with a short path and time-to-seal, rather than making every crack compete for attention. A successful seal should visibly stop the grain and give one crisp relief sound. Do not automatically erase all damage: the player's repair action should matter.

Make pressure oscillate between “I see a problem,” “I choose a response,” and “that worked.” Use fixed live caps and arrival groups for the first implementation. If caps delay a group, do not dump the accumulated queue all at once later. Never spawn behind the player at close range to meet a timing target. Tune spawn distance along a valid route and preserve entrance warnings.

**Validation that can establish fun**

Run an initial round with eight fresh players, mixed between less experienced and experienced action-game players. Use clean profiles, no debug assists, normal speed, and both keyboard/mouse and gamepad. Counterbalance the current opening and the proposed opening to reduce order effects. Record actions and ask brief questions between waves; do not interrupt fights with a survey. Use this small round diagnostically, not as a statistically proven retention result.

Starting acceptance targets:

- At least 7/8 identify the objective and their next useful action without coaching; at least 6/8 can identify and use a taught ladder/roof/return route when prompted by a relevant tactical opportunity. Record whether they voluntarily choose it and why; rooftop use alone is not proof it is fun.
- First meaningful combat arrives within 20 seconds of gaining control in the revised prologue. The first real wave offers an attack opportunity within six seconds after the siren.
- No accidental combat lull over eight seconds with enemies still scheduled; intentional respite is telegraphed. Cleared queues never force the player to wait out a timer.
- Most active preparation takes 15–35 seconds, excluding deliberate paused browsing. Nobody loses grain while reading a paused shop.
- Median self-reported stress rises across the arc, roughly 2/5 → 3/5 → 3/5 → 4/5 → 4/5, and falls after each clear. Treat ratings as design feedback, not precision measurements.
- Aim initially for at least 7/8 clearing wave one and 5/8 clearing wave five on their first attempt; investigate the cause of each failure rather than just reducing all damage.
- At least 6/8 can name a favorite moment without prompting; at least 5/8 choose to continue or retry when genuinely offered a stopping point. Watch their behavior as well as their answers.
- Complete all five waves and audit every rooftop/repair route with flight disabled. Test enemy/telegraph readability in Morning/Clear first, then every supported lighting/weather combination. Capture normal-speed frame times around kills, crowd collisions, climbing, zipline transitions, and the boss; the accelerated audit screenshots are not a performance benchmark.

Use targeted automated regressions for reward idempotence/save migration, queue exhaustion, live caps, guaranteed introductions, held shopping timers, and reachable teaching routes. Repeat schedule checks across 100 seeds. Preserve the existing tutorial, weapon, save/wave, and content-table checks, updating assertions for intentional changes. The audit fixture does not establish that those suites pass.

**First implementation slice:** authored wave one, immediate queue-based completion, capped ready reward, and the pistol kit/quick-buy transition. Play that short loop before building the rest. Then tune wave three around existing climbing and firing routes, followed by the wave-four remix and Foreman. Develop the later jetpack gameplay as a separate prototype with the gates in [its plan](JETPACK_GAMEPLAY_AND_REWARD_PLAN.md); do not use an earlier unlock to fill an opening pacing gap.
